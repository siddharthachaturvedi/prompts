# Claims audit

A claim becomes more credible when it is named accurately. "Contributor to the report" beats "author of the report" if contributor is what the record shows. A reader who finds one inflated line discounts every other line.

This skill checks claims one at a time, then proposes the smallest true wording for each. It changes nothing public until the owner says so.

## Three questions for every claim

Judge each on its own evidence. Record all three before proposing any wording.

### Source - who says so?

Grade the best source you can find.

| Grade | What it is | How far to trust it |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent** | An institution's own page, report or publication that names the person | Strong |
| **Hosted** | A speaker page or event biography. The organizer hosts it, the person often wrote it | Shows the relationship, not every detail |
| **Self** | The person's own résumé, profile or site | Says how they describe it. Not verification |
| **None** | Nothing public | Hold, or keep it private |

Several résumés that agree are still one source. They descend from the same draft.

### Scope - what exactly does the source support?

Name the relationship. Each rung is a legitimate experience. None needs to be inflated into the one above it.

- **Work:** employed, contracted, founded, co-founded, advised, member of a group.
- **Writing:** author, co-author, contributor, acknowledged, interviewed.
- **Speaking:** spoke, panelist, listed as speaker, attended.
- **Recognition:** won, team award, shortlisted, nominated.
- **Study:** degree, course or program, in progress.

Watch for these traps:

- **A team award is not an individual award.**
- **A program is not a degree.**
- **A nominee list is not a winner list.**
- **An acknowledgment is not an author credit.**
- **Working with an institution is not being employed by it.**

### Number - whose is it, and as of when?

Every number needs a date and a denominator.

- **Whose money?** Program funding is not personal revenue or money personally raised.
- **Which count?** "Evaluated", "built" and "in production" are three different numbers.
- **Which version?** Where versions of a résumé disagree, use only the figure a source supports.

## From three answers to one verdict

| Verdict | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Publish** | Independent or hosted source. Wording matches the scope | Keep it, and link the source |
| **Reword** | The experience is real, the wording overstates it | Propose the smallest true sentence |
| **Hold** | Plausible, but it needs a fact only the owner has | Ask. Leave it off until answered |
| **Drop** | No source, and it adds little | Remove it. Say what was removed |

## Logos and marks

A logo is a claim with no words. It cannot say what the relationship was.

- **Marks for employers and schools** are common practice. Use each one unaltered, and record where the file came from.
- **Many public bodies restrict their marks.** Health services, governments and United Nations bodies often require permission. Use the name as text, with the role beside it.
- **Prefer words wherever the relationship needs explaining.** "Advisory group member" says more than a logo can.

## Workflow

1. **Scope.** Which surface is being audited, and who reads it.
2. **Inventory.** One row per claim, copied exactly as written. Include numbers, titles, dates and logos.
3. **Find sources.** Start from links the person supplies. Prefer the page that names the person over a general homepage.
4. **Grade and classify.** Fill in source, scope and number for each row.
5. **Reconcile versions.** Where documents disagree, list the variants side by side. Do not average them.
6. **Propose wording.** One line per claim, with its source link.
7. **Ask.** Give the owner the Hold list as short questions, each one answerable in a line.
8. **Record.** Keep a sources file beside the page: each claim, its source, and the date checked.
9. **Recheck.** Links rot and event pages roll over each year. Date every check.

## Privacy

Searching for a person by name sends that name to outside services. Ask before you do it. Résumés hold phone numbers and addresses. Never copy those into notes, logs or public files.

## Report back

- The table: claim, source grade, scope, verdict, proposed wording, link.
- The Hold list, as questions.
- What you could not check, and why.

Say plainly that an unverified claim is not a false one. Public search cannot establish private business results.

## What this skill cannot do

- It cannot verify what was never made public.
- It is not legal advice on trademarks or endorsements. Check an institution's own rules before using its mark.
- It does not decide what the person wants to be known for. It only keeps what they say accurate.
